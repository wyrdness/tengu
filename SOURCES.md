# Sources & References

## Historical Texts

### Primary Sources
- **Nihon Shoki** (Chronicles of Japan), 720 CE. First written mention of tengu in Japan.
- **Konjaku Monogatarishū** (今昔物語集), 12th century. Medieval Japanese tale collection featuring tengu stories.
- **Tengu-zōshi emaki** (天狗草紙絵巻), 13th-14th century. Illustrated scrolls depicting tengu and Buddhist narratives.

## Academic Works

### Folklore Studies
- Yanagita, Kunio. (Early 20th century). *Japanese Folklore Studies*. Pioneer of Japanese folklore documentation, including tengu traditions.
- De Visser, M.W. (1908). "The Tengu." *Transactions of the Asiatic Society of Japan*, Vol. 36, Part 2.
- Reider, Noriko T. (2010). *Japanese Demon Lore: Oni from Ancient Times to the Present*. Utah State University Press.

### Buddhist & Religious Studies
- Faure, Bernard. (2015). *Gods of Medieval Japan* (Volume 2: Protectors and Predators). University of Hawaii Press.
- Kanda, Christine Guth. (1985). "Tengu: The Legendary Mountain Goblin of Japan." *Japan Quarterly*, 32(2).

## Cultural Studies

### Japanese Mythology
- Ashkenazi, Michael. (2003). *Handbook of Japanese Mythology*. ABC-CLIO.
- Roberts, Jeremy. (2009). *Japanese Mythology A to Z*. Chelsea House Publishers.

## Visual & Artistic Sources

### Art & Iconography
- Museum collections of tengu masks and artwork (Tokyo National Museum, Kyoto National Museum)
- Ukiyo-e prints featuring tengu
- Noh and Kyogen theater mask traditions

## Modern Sources

### Popular Culture Studies
- Napier, Susan J. (2005). *Anime from Akira to Howl's Moving Dragon: Experiencing Contemporary Japanese Animation*. Palgrave Macmillan.
- Analysis of tengu in modern anime, manga, and video games

### Religious & Cultural Context
- Shinto shrine documentation (Mount Kurama, Mount Takao)
- Buddhist temple records
- Yamabushi (mountain ascetic) tradition studies

## Martial Arts Connections

### Martial Arts Mythology
- Draeger, Donn F. & Smith, Robert W. (1980). *Comprehensive Asian Fighting Arts*. Kodansha International.
- Connections between tengu legends and martial arts traditions
- Sōjōbō and Minamoto no Yoshitsune legends
